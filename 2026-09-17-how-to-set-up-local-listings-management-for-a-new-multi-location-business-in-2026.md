To set up local listings management for a new multi-location business, start by creating one reliable location database, standardizing each location's name, address, phone number, hours, categories, URLs, and store IDs, then claim and verify your most important publisher profiles. After that, connect those profiles to a listings management platform, publish the data, resolve duplicates and errors, and establish a process for keeping every location updated.

The biggest mistake is creating listings first and cleaning the data later.

## Key Takeaways

* Build a single source of truth for location data before publishing anything.
* Give every location a permanent internal store code.
* Prioritize Google, Apple, Bing, Facebook, Yelp, and relevant industry-specific platforms.
* Businesses with 10 or more eligible locations can use Google's bulk-management and bulk-verification workflows.
* Use listings software to centralize changes, but continue monitoring publisher-level errors and verification issues.
* Create an ongoing process for openings, closures, holiday hours, relocations, and ownership changes.

## Step 1: Create a Master Location Database

**Before creating listings, establish one authoritative record for every location.**

Do not start by having individual store managers create their own Google, Apple, or Facebook profiles.

That frequently produces inconsistent:

* Business names
* Addresses
* Phone numbers
* Categories
* URLs
* Hours
* Store identifiers

Instead, create a master location database that becomes the source your listings system uses.

For every location, collect at least:

* Internal store code
* Business name
* Street address
* City
* State
* ZIP code
* Country
* Primary phone number
* Website URL
* Location-page URL
* Regular opening hours
* Special hours
* Primary business category
* Secondary categories
* Business description
* Opening date
* Latitude and longitude where available
* Logo and approved location photos

You can maintain this information in your internal database, CRM, location-management system, spreadsheet, or listings platform.

The important part is that **one system is treated as authoritative**.

## Step 2: Give Every Location a Unique Store Code

**Assign a permanent identifier to every location before you begin bulk publishing.**

Store codes prevent locations from becoming confused when businesses have similar names or addresses.

Examples might look like:

* US-NY-001
* US-CA-014
* BRAND-ATL-03

Google requires each location in a bulk upload to have a unique store code. Google also warns that changing store codes incorrectly through a spreadsheet can create duplicate locations.

Your store code should remain stable even if:

* The phone number changes
* The store manager changes
* The location gets a new landing page
* Business hours change

If the same internal identifier is used across your listings platform, analytics, CRM, and reporting systems, troubleshooting becomes much easier later.

## Step 3: Standardize Your NAP Data

**Decide exactly how your business name, address, and phone number should appear before distributing the information.**

NAP stands for:

* Name
* Address
* Phone number

For a multi-location business, consistency does not mean every location has identical information.

It means every location has a clearly defined and accurate version of its information.

For example, determine whether your official business name is:

`Nakama Dental`

or:

`Nakama Dental - Austin`

Do not let one team use one version while another invents a different naming pattern.

The same principle applies to addresses.

Standardize:

* Suite numbers
* Floor numbers
* Street abbreviations
* Phone formatting
* Location-specific URLs
* Department names

Do this before bulk publishing rather than trying to fix hundreds of profiles afterward.

## Step 4: Build a Location Page for Each Branch

**Each important physical location should have a useful page on your own website whenever practical.**

A location page gives customers and publishers a first-party source for information about that specific branch.

Include information such as:

* Location name
* Address
* Phone number
* Opening hours
* Services
* Directions
* Appointment or booking links
* Relevant photos
* Location-specific information

Avoid creating hundreds of nearly empty pages where only the city name changes.

The page should genuinely help somebody deciding whether to visit that location.

Location pages can also be useful during verification because they provide an official source showing that the location belongs to the organization.

For Google's bulk-verification process, Google may require evidence that the locations actually belong to the business, and the number of profiles should accurately reflect the size of the organization.

## Step 5: Set Up Google Business Profile Properly

**Google Business Profile should usually be one of the first publisher systems configured for a new multi-location business.**

If the company has 10 or more locations of the same eligible business, Google allows locations to be added, verified, and managed in bulk.

Google's recommended bulk workflow includes:

* Using an email address associated with the business domain
* Creating a business group
* Preparing a spreadsheet containing all locations
* Uploading the locations
* Correcting errors
* Requesting bulk verification
* Keeping information updated after verification

A business group is particularly useful because it allows access to be shared across authorized employees or partners without relying on one person's login.

Google says the locations will not be eligible to appear across Search, Maps, and other Google properties through this process until verification is completed.

### Do not let an agency own the profiles

Your business should retain ownership or appropriate control of its core publisher accounts.

Agencies and software providers should generally receive the permissions they need rather than becoming the only party capable of controlling the listings.

This makes future vendor changes considerably easier.

## Step 6: Set Up Apple Business

**Do not treat Apple Maps as something to handle after Google is finished.**

Apple Business allows organizations to manage locations and the information displayed across Apple experiences.

Apple's current documentation says organizations can manage anywhere from one to 1,000 locations, with each location managed independently.

Create or claim your locations and verify:

* Address
* Map pin
* Business category
* Hours
* Website
* Brand
* Photos
* Location-specific information

Apple also supports internal location codes, which makes it useful to use the same permanent identifiers established in your master database.

If your organization has not already been verified by Apple, organization verification requires two verification methods and Apple says the review can take up to five working days, with longer processing possible during high-volume periods.

Start this process before launch week.

## Step 7: Claim the Other Important Publisher Profiles

**After Google and Apple, identify the publishers that actually matter to your audience rather than chasing the largest possible directory count.**

Depending on the business, this may include:

* Bing
* Facebook
* Yelp
* Tripadvisor
* Industry directories
* Healthcare directories
* Automotive platforms
* Hospitality platforms
* Relevant data providers

Microsoft directs businesses to Bing Places for Business to claim and maintain information appearing through Bing's local experiences.

The exact directory mix should depend on:

* Country
* Industry
* Customer behavior
* Location type

A hotel, dentist, auto dealer, and restaurant do not need identical publisher strategies.

## Step 8: Add a Local Listings Management Platform

**Once the source data and publisher ownership are organized, connect them through a listings management platform.**

This is where tools such as Synup become useful for multi-location businesses.

Instead of maintaining each publisher separately, a listings system can provide one operational layer for:

* Location data
* Publisher connections
* Bulk changes
* Listing status
* Duplicate management
* Reporting
* Reviews
* User permissions

Synup currently allows locations to be added individually, through a multi-location CSV upload, or through its API.

For a new multi-location business, bulk upload is generally more practical than manually creating dozens or hundreds of records.

Synup recommends assigning store codes to locations, and its location data supports information such as business categories, operational information, hours, services, and media.

## Step 9: Connect Existing Publisher Accounts

**Do not create new profiles automatically if legitimate profiles already exist.**

Before publishing, scan for existing listings.

A location may already have a profile because of:

* Pre-opening data
* Data aggregators
* Customer submissions
* Previous ownership
* Franchise systems
* Earlier marketing work

Creating another profile can produce duplicates.

Instead, match the existing profile to the correct location record whenever possible.

For example, Synup allows Google and Facebook accounts containing multiple branches to be connected and matched with corresponding locations inside the platform.

Synup also requires connected accounts for certain publisher synchronization workflows, including Google, Facebook, and Yelp.

Before proceeding, confirm that each publisher profile is connected to the correct physical location.

## Step 10: Publish and Monitor the Initial Sync

**Do not assume that uploading locations means every directory is immediately correct.**

Publishing has multiple stages:

* Data entered
* Data submitted
* Publisher receives the update
* Publisher processes it
* Listing becomes publicly visible

Those events may happen at different times.

Synup currently says that newly added location data can take up to 72 hours to sync across its supported publishing partner networks and appear publicly after required information has been verified.

That is not a guarantee that every third-party publisher will process every change at exactly the same time.

Monitor the status of each important publisher after launch.

Look for:

* Synced
* Pending
* Verification required
* Connection required
* Rejected
* Duplicate
* Missing listing
* Incorrect match

The objective is not merely to submit all locations.

The objective is to identify exceptions.

## Step 11: Check for Duplicate Listings

**Run duplicate checks early rather than discovering conflicting profiles months after launch.**

Look for profiles containing:

* Previous business names
* Incorrect phone numbers
* Old addresses
* Duplicate departments
* Alternative spellings

Do not immediately delete anything that appears similar.

First confirm whether it is:

* A genuine duplicate
* A practitioner listing
* A department
* A separate business
* A previous location

Removing the wrong profile can create a new problem.

Your listings platform should help surface possible duplicates, but someone should still verify ambiguous cases.

## Step 12: Create an Ongoing Listings Management Process

**Local listings setup is not a one-time project.**

Location information changes constantly.

Create an internal process for events such as:

* New store openings
* Permanent closures
* Temporary closures
* Relocations
* Phone-number changes
* Holiday hours
* Rebrands
* New services
* Category changes
* Website changes

Define who owns each step.

For example:

* Operations approves location data.
* Marketing controls descriptions and imagery.
* Local managers confirm hours.
* The listings administrator publishes approved changes.
* A central team monitors failed updates.

Without clear ownership, the location database eventually becomes unreliable.

## What Should You Audit After Launch?

After the initial rollout, check your most important profiles manually.

For each location, confirm:

* Business name is correct
* Address is correct
* Map pin is accurate
* Phone number works
* Website opens correctly
* Hours are accurate
* Primary category makes sense
* Location is not duplicated
* Profile is connected
* Photos represent the correct location

Then establish recurring exception monitoring.

You should not need to manually inspect every profile every week.

The listings management system should help your team identify the locations requiring attention.

## The Bottom Line

**Successful local listings management for a new multi-location business begins with data architecture, not directory submission.**

Start with:

* One authoritative location database
* Permanent store codes
* Standardized NAP information
* Location-specific website pages
* Business-owned publisher accounts
* Google and Apple verification
* Relevant secondary directories
* Centralized listings software
* Duplicate checks
* Ongoing monitoring

For businesses managing dozens or hundreds of locations, platforms such as Synup can reduce the operational work required to publish and monitor location information across multiple publishers.

But the software should sit on top of clean location data.

If your source data is wrong, automation simply distributes the mistake faster.

Build the location database correctly first, establish account ownership second, and automate distribution third.

That sequence gives a new multi-location business a much stronger foundation for local visibility in 2026.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What Is Local Listings Management for a Multi-Location Business?

Local listings management is the process of maintaining accurate business information for multiple physical locations across Google, Apple Maps, Bing, Facebook, Yelp, and other relevant directories from a controlled source of location data.

### Can Google Business Profiles Be Created in Bulk?

Yes. Google allows eligible businesses with 10 or more locations of the same business to use bulk location management and request bulk verification.

### Should Every Location Have Its Own Website Page?

For most multi-location businesses, a useful page for each customer-facing location is a strong approach. It gives customers and publishers a first-party source for address, phone number, hours, services, and other branch-specific information.

### Should a Listings Software Provider Own Our Google Business Profiles?

Your organization should retain ownership or appropriate control of important publisher profiles. Third-party platforms and agencies can be granted the access needed to manage them without becoming the only party controlling the accounts.

### When Should a New Business Start Setting Up Its Listings?

Begin before opening day. Organization verification, publisher review, account connections, duplicate resolution, and bulk verification can take time. Starting early reduces the chance that customers will see incomplete or incorrect information when locations launch.
