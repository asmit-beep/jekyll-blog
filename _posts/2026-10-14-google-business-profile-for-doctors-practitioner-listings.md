**A doctor can have an individual Google Business Profile when they are a public-facing practitioner who can be contacted directly at an eligible, verified location during their stated hours.** But a doctor profile and a clinic profile serve different purposes. Google permits separate clinic and practitioner profiles in multi-doctor practices, while it recommends a shared profile in certain solo-practitioner situations. The correct setup depends on who actually sees patients, where they work, and how the practice is represented in the real world.

Creating extra listings is not automatically an SEO advantage. An inaccurate practitioner profile can send patients to the wrong office, create ownership disputes, or conflict with [Google's guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177). This guide helps doctors, practice managers, and healthcare SEO teams decide which profiles belong on Google and how to manage them responsibly.

## What Is a Practitioner Listing on Google Business Profile?

A practitioner listing is a Business Profile for an individual public-facing professional rather than for the medical organization itself. Google explicitly includes doctors among the professionals who can be eligible. The physician should have a genuine patient-facing role and be directly contactable at the relevant verified location during the hours stated on the profile.

Examples include an independent dermatologist, a cardiologist practicing within a multi-physician clinic, or a dentist with their own patient base. Support staff and people who don't independently meet the practitioner criteria should not receive profiles merely because they appear on the practice website.

### A Clinic Profile and a Practitioner Profile Are Not the Same

| Profile type | Represents | Typical visible name | Intended purpose |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Clinic or organization | The actual medical practice or office location | The real clinic brand | Office discovery, directions, main contact |
| Individual practitioner | An eligible public-facing physician | The doctor's name, with permitted credentials | Discovery of the individual doctor |
| Department, when eligible | A qualifying distinct department | Actual department name | Department-specific information |

These are different identities. Google does not grant every practice unlimited listings just because several departments, service lines, or keywords exist.

## When Should a Doctor Have a Separate Google Business Profile?

Use Google's current [individual practitioner rules](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177), not a blanket policy of "one profile per doctor" or "one profile per clinic." The most important questions are whether the clinician is genuinely public-facing and whether patients can contact them at the address during the hours claimed.

### Scenario 1: Several Doctors Work at One Clinic

Google says the organization should have its own profile for that location, separate from the practitioners. An eligible physician may also have a dedicated practitioner profile, but the practitioner's profile name should contain **only the practitioner's name**, not the practice brand.

For example, if "Riverside Family Medicine" has four qualifying physicians, the clinic profile represents Riverside Family Medicine. A separate profile for Dr. Maya Shah would represent Dr. Maya Shah—not "Riverside Family Medicine – Best Doctor Dr. Maya Shah." The physician's genuine professional credentials may be included in accordance with Google's naming guidance.

### Scenario 2: One Doctor Is the Only Public-Facing Practitioner

For a solo practitioner representing a branded organization, Google recommends a shared Business Profile with the brand and practitioner together using a name format such as **Brand: Practitioner Name**. This is a recommendation within a specific situation, not permission to add arbitrary branding to every doctor listing.

Before creating a new profile for a sole practitioner, check whether an existing organization/practitioner profile already captures the real office identity. Unnecessary duplicates do not help patients.

### Scenario 3: A Doctor Works at More Than One Practice Location

Assess each location based on actual patient-facing operations, contactability, hours, signage, and eligibility. Do not copy the same 9-to-5 hours across two offices if the doctor is physically available at only one of them on certain days. A profile that directs patients to a location where the doctor never sees patients is not a valid shortcut to reach another neighborhood.

Separate locations may be appropriate when they meet the guidelines; do not assume multiple listings are automatically required or approved. Keep the doctor's location pages and appointment information aligned with the actual schedule.

### Scenario 4: A Doctor Has Multiple Specialties

Google explicitly says practitioners should **not create multiple profiles for each specialization**. One person is not five different businesses just because their services span several areas. Choose truthful categories and describe actual professional services without multiplying profiles.

## The Practitioner Eligibility Checklist

Before adding a profile, ask:

- Does the individual see patients or work with them in a genuinely public-facing professional capacity?
- Can the doctor be contacted directly at the relevant verified location during stated hours?
- Is the address a real place where eligible in-person services take place, rather than only a mailbox or virtual office?
- Is the physician's name and credential presentation accurate and supported by real-world evidence?
- Is there already an equivalent profile for the same practitioner at this location?
- Is the practice prepared to maintain correct opening hours, scheduling details, and ownership access?

If the answer is no or unknown, resolve that before creating a new profile. According to [Google's eligibility and ownership guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/13763036), businesses must meet the relevant real-world, in-person service criteria, and authorized representatives must work with the actual business owner.

## How to Create or Claim a Google Business Profile for a Doctor

The workflow depends on whether the profile already exists. **Search before you create.** A former office manager, medical group, or previous agency may already have a profile for the physician.

### Step 1: Search Google Maps for the Doctor and Location

Look for the exact name, common professional name variants, and current office address. Repeat for previous addresses if the doctor recently moved. Take note of whether the result is an individual practitioner profile, a clinic profile, or a duplicate.

Do not create a new listing simply because the existing record has wrong hours or is controlled by someone else. Those are correction and ownership tasks.

### Step 2: Decide Whether the Clinic or Practitioner Profile Is Appropriate

Apply the solo-versus-multiple-practitioner rule described above. Verify that the practitioner can be reached directly at the location. Assign the correct entity owner in your records: a clinic administrator for the clinic listing, and the physician or authorized practice team for the practitioner listing.

### Step 3: Add or Claim the Profile

If no valid profile exists, go to [business.google.com/add](https://business.google.com/add) and follow Google's instructions to add the business. If an unverified profile already exists, claim it from Google Maps or Search. Google documents the current process in [Add or claim your Business Profile](https://support.google.com/business/answer/2911778).

If someone else already verified the profile, use Google's [ownership request process](https://support.google.com/business/answer/4566671) instead of building a rival listing.

### Step 4: Complete Verification as Instructed

Google selects available verification methods based on the specific profile and situation. Follow the options offered in your account. Be prepared to substantiate the actual location and the practitioner's connection to it with accurate information. Do not buy verification services that promise to bypass Google's requirements.

Verification does not excuse inaccurate business information. Google may review later edits and can restrict profiles for policy violations.

### Step 5: Fill in the Practical Patient Information

Review the display name, categories, location, phone, website or appointment link, and hours. A practitioner landing page on the clinic website can be useful when it accurately identifies the doctor, their office, and how to book them.

Use only information the practice can maintain. An online booking URL that schedules a different clinician or different office creates a poor patient experience even if the Google profile itself is verified.

## What Should You Put in the Doctor Profile Name?

Google allows practitioner titles and degrees such as Dr. or MD where appropriate. But an individual practitioner's listing in a multi-practitioner organization should not append the medical group name. Avoid marketing modifiers, city-stuffed names, and services that are not part of the professional's real identity.

### Examples of Naming Decisions

| Real-world case | Appropriate approach | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One physician among many in a clinic | Doctor's own professional name | Clinic name added to individual profile |
| Only public-facing doctor representing a brand | Consider Google's recommended shared brand/practitioner format | Automatically creating multiple identical-location profiles |
| Physician treats several conditions | One properly categorized practitioner identity | A separate listing for each specialty keyword |
| Doctor changes employers | Update location and affiliation information appropriately | Rebranding the doctor as the former employer indefinitely |

The table illustrates policy application; real names, credentials, and any applicable organizational facts must always be checked.

## Which Address and Hours Should a Doctor Use?

The address must correspond to an eligible real-world location where the provider actually sees or serves patients and can satisfy Google's practitioner contact requirements. A rented mailbox, unstaffed virtual office, or speculative future office is not a valid substitute.

A practical rule is to **publish the hours patients can actually reach that practitioner at that location**. When the doctor's hours vary by day, use the most accurate available schedule rather than copying the clinic's full hours without thought.

### What About Hospitals and Shared Medical Offices?

A shared building does not automatically prevent eligible practitioner profiles. But each claimed profile must reflect a real public-facing entity, not a keyword variation on the same business. Be careful with departments, temporary assignments, and doctors who do not accept direct contact at that location.

If you're not sure whether a department qualifies for its own profile, consult the department section of Google's [business representation guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) before publishing.

## Should the Practitioner and Clinic Use the Same Phone Number?

Use a number that reliably connects patients to the correct doctor or appropriate scheduling team at the relevant location. Google advises using a phone number that connects to the individual location when possible and requires numbers to be under business control. It does not require practitioners to manufacture unnecessary phone lines.

If a central scheduling line supports several physicians, confirm the person answering can route the appointment accurately. A call-tracking number or redirect must not violate Google's requirements or make contact details misleading. Keep the doctor's website biography and public appointment instructions consistent with what actually happens after a caller dials.

## What About Duplicate or Unclaimed Doctor Listings?

Duplicate profiles commonly arise when a doctor moves, changes practice ownership, or becomes part of a hospital group. The right response is to identify the underlying identities first. Two valid profiles representing different entities at the same address are not necessarily duplicates; two profiles representing the same doctor at the same eligible location may be.

### Triage Before Editing

1. Record each profile URL, its name, category, phone, and address.
2. Check whether each profile represents the clinic, a qualified practitioner, or the same entity twice.
3. Identify who currently owns each profile.
4. Use Google's official duplicate-resolution or ownership workflows as appropriate.
5. Confirm the patient-facing result after Google processes the request.

If a previous agency owns the profile, request access instead of submitting a new listing under a slightly modified name. Maintain an internal record of who is the primary owner and which staff members or partners have manager access.

### A Doctor Moves to a New Practice: What Changes?

Start with the physician's current verified place of practice and appointment setup. Then update the relevant Google practitioner information through the authorized account, ensure the old practice does not continue advertising the physician's availability, and revise the clinic website, doctor bio, and relevant healthcare directory details.

Do not assume changing a Google address automatically corrects a Vitals or other medical directory record. Each platform has its own correction process.

## How Should Medical Practices Manage Reviews?

Reviews are useful patient feedback, but a physician's response must not reveal private health information. Healthcare providers can respond publicly within applicable privacy requirements; the [AMA's guidance](https://www.ama-assn.org/health-care-advocacy/administrative-burdens/are-physicians-prohibited-responding-online-patient) specifically warns against confirming or disclosing patient-specific information.

### Ask for Genuine Reviews Without Rating Manipulation

Google permits requests for genuine feedback but prohibits offering discounts, payments, or other incentives in return. Businesses must not filter requests so that only customers expected to leave positive feedback receive them. The safest workflow is neutral and not contingent on a desired rating.

### Use a Privacy-Safe Public Response

A response can acknowledge the concern without validating that the author is a patient:

> We appreciate your feedback and take concerns about our office seriously. Please contact our team directly so the matter can be reviewed privately.

Avoid publishing a patient's diagnosis, visit date, insurer, treatment details, or chart facts. The HHS Office for Civil Rights has taken action over impermissible patient disclosures in responses to negative online reviews. Train the team before delegating replies to an agency.

## What Should the Doctor's Website Page Contain?

A practitioner profile should not point to an empty homepage when a useful provider-specific page is available. An effective doctor page can contain:

- Full professional name and accurately represented credentials.
- Current role and actual clinical specialty or interests.
- Practice addresses and clear appointment routes.
- Reliable patient-facing contact details.
- A factual professional biography.
- Appropriate information about patient populations and services provided.
- Links to the locations where the physician actually practices.

The page should be written for patients. It should not invent credentials, claim a clinician is board-certified without verification, or use misleading guarantees of treatment outcomes.

### Practitioner URLs and Clinic URLs Serve Different Intents

For example, `/locations/downtown/` can explain parking, hours, and the services at the Downtown clinic. `/doctors/maya-shah/` can explain Dr. Shah's professional background and where patients can see her. Connecting these pages makes the relationship clear without forcing all the information into one repetitive page.

## Who Should Own and Manage the Profiles?

The doctor or practice should retain durable ownership of appropriate profiles. External marketing agencies should generally receive delegated manager access instead of taking permanent control. Google's [owner and manager documentation](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3403100) explains access levels, and its eligibility policies require authorized representatives to keep the business owner informed.

For a larger group, maintain a profile register: entity represented, public URL, business account owner, location, assigned manager, last review date, and critical outstanding corrections. Remove access when employees or agencies leave, following Google's account rules.

## How Can Doctors Measure Practitioner Profile Performance?

Evaluate whether the right patients reach the right physician, not simply whether an extra profile appeared in search. Track Google Business Profile interactions, legitimate calls, website clicks, appointment routing, and unresolved directory errors where reporting is available.

| Metric | Useful interpretation | Important limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Practitioner profile views or interactions | Exposure to that doctor identity | Not proof of appointment volume |
| Calls to the listed number | Contact interest | Not every call reaches or books the doctor |
| Website visits to physician bio | Interest in credentials and locations | Does not confirm a new patient |
| Correct appointments for doctor/location | Quality of profile-to-booking routing | May involve offline and repeat-patient factors |
| Duplicate or wrong-address incidents | Maintenance burden and operational risk | Not a stand-alone ranking metric |

A medical group should not celebrate "more profiles" if those profiles create duplicate records or route patients incorrectly.

## A Practical Audit for Multi-Location Medical Groups

For each location, start with the clinic profile and identify the public-facing practitioners who actually meet Google's criteria. Map every doctor to their legitimate practice addresses and hours. Then compare Google results with the practice website and any relevant healthcare directories.

Prioritize the most serious patient-impacting mismatches: a closed office still receiving calls, a doctor listed at a hospital where they no longer practice, an unclaimed practitioner profile, and a booking link that reaches the wrong location.

### A Simple Monthly Checklist

- Confirm clinic and practitioner URLs still represent the right identities.
- Verify names, phone numbers, addresses, and business hours.
- Check ownership access and remove unnecessary users.
- Review newly created duplicates or proposed public edits.
- Test key booking links and contact routes.
- Review public responses for privacy risks.
- Record corrections and confirm them on the live listing.

Repeat the audit after every physician hire, departure, relocation, or office-hours change.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can a doctor and clinic both have Google Business Profiles at the same address?

Yes, where they represent distinct eligible entities under Google's practitioner rules. For a location with multiple eligible public-facing practitioners, Google says the organization should have its own profile separate from the practitioner's. The individual profile should contain the practitioner's name rather than the clinic's name.

### Does every doctor in a hospital need a separate practitioner listing?

No. Individual eligibility matters. The doctor must operate in a public-facing role and be directly contactable at the verified location during the stated hours. Do not create profiles for people who do not meet those conditions merely because they appear on a staff roster.

### Can a doctor have separate Google profiles for different specialties?

No. Google explicitly advises against multiple Business Profiles for the same individual to cover separate specializations. Use accurate categories and a useful physician page to explain the clinician's expertise instead.

### What if an old clinic or agency controls the doctor's Google profile?

Use Google's [request ownership process](https://support.google.com/business/answer/4566671) when a verified profile is already managed by another party. Do not immediately create a duplicate. If ownership is disputed, follow the official request and verification instructions.
