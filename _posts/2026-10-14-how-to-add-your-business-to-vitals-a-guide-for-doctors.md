If you want your practice to appear accurately on Vitals, **start by searching for the physician's existing profile, then claim it or request a correction**. Vitals is a physician-focused directory, not a general-purpose business listing site: a doctor's professional record and the practice locations attached to it are the key pieces. Physicians and their staff can use Vitals' official [claim form](https://go.vitals.com/vitals/doctor/claim/). If a listing has incorrect or outdated details, use [Vitals' feedback channel](https://www.vitals.com/feedback).

A listing that appears automatically is not necessarily a listing you control. That distinction matters when a doctor has moved offices, joined a new group, changed a phone number, or been shown as inactive despite continuing to practice.

This guide covers the right workflow for U.S. doctors, practice managers, and medical-group marketing teams. It also explains what Vitals can—and cannot—do for local visibility.

## What Is Vitals, and Can a Medical Practice Be Added?

Vitals is a U.S. healthcare directory where people research physicians and other healthcare professionals. Its database can contain physician records even before the physician personally claims a profile. Vitals says its information comes from public and private sources, including state medical boards, federal websites, hospitals, doctors, and other partners.

That means **"add my business to Vitals" is usually the wrong starting assumption**. You might be trying to solve one of three different problems:

1. Your physician is already on Vitals, but the address, phone number, name, or practice association is wrong.
2. Your physician exists on Vitals but hasn't claimed the profile, so no one at the practice manages its information.
3. You cannot find a relevant physician profile and need to contact Vitals about creating or locating an appropriate record.

Vitals displays physician information and may also show group practices or hospitals. A medical group's brand is not interchangeable with an individual doctor's professional listing. Don't invent a second doctor profile just to create another marketing entry for the office.

### Choose the Correct Route Before You Start

| Your situation | Best next action | What to avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Doctor profile found, details correct | Claim the existing physician profile if eligible | Creating a competing or duplicate physician record |
| Profile found, practice address wrong | Claim or contact Vitals with evidence of the current practice address | Assuming a Google edit will update Vitals automatically |
| Doctor incorrectly shown as inactive | Follow the claim path and confirm licensure and patient-accessible practice address | Treating an inactive flag as simply a marketing setting |
| Doctor cannot be found | Search alternate name/specialty/location, then contact Vitals | Submitting unverified personal or credential details |
| Group practice needs visibility | Check physician records and any existing group-practice entries | Claiming a physician identity as though it were a generic business page |

**Source:** [Vitals' official FAQ](https://www.vitals.com/faq) describes its sources, inactive-profile logic, and correction path.

## How Do You Find an Existing Doctor Profile on Vitals?

Search Vitals using the physician's full professional name and current city. Search again using a specialty, an earlier professional name if relevant, and a broader location if the first attempt fails. A common name may produce several people; don't select a record solely because the specialty matches.

Before claiming anything, compare the profile against facts your practice controls:

- The physician's name, including any appropriate professional credentials.
- Medical specialty and whether the listing describes the actual services performed.
- Office address, suite, city, state, and ZIP code.
- Practice or appointment telephone number.
- Current organization or group affiliation, where displayed.
- Active or inactive status.
- A photograph or other details that could misidentify the provider.

For a practice with several physicians, create a simple inventory with one row per physician and the URL of the profile you found. This is more reliable than trying to remember which listings were edited.

### What If There Are Several Doctors With the Same Name?

Match using a combination of location, specialty, known practice affiliations, and available credentials. If identification remains ambiguous, ask Vitals for help rather than claiming the nearest match. A wrongly claimed physician profile can confuse patients and create a more difficult correction later.

### What If the Doctor Is Not Listed?

Vitals notes that physicians may be missing because of recent licensure, relocation, name changes, or delays in its sources. Search broadly before assuming a profile must be created. Then use the official claim/contact channels and provide only accurate, verifiable information.

## How to Claim Your Physician Profile on Vitals

Vitals currently provides a public [Claim Your Physician Profile form](https://go.vitals.com/vitals/doctor/claim/). It states that **only physicians or their staff members are permitted to claim profiles**. The form requests contact information including email, first and last name, company, telephone number, and comments. It does not publicly guarantee immediate approval or a specific turnaround time.

### Step 1: Prepare Your Physician and Practice Information

Gather a verified reference record before starting:

- Doctor's exact professional name and current credentials.
- Current practice location(s), including suite numbers.
- Main scheduling or office telephone number.
- Practice name and website.
- Official work email for the physician or an authorized staff member.
- License or other professional identifying information if requested during follow-up.
- URLs and screenshots identifying incorrect profile information, when relevant.

Use publicly verifiable professional data, not patient records. If your practice is part of a larger health system, confirm who has authority to request profile changes.

### Step 2: Open the Official Claim Form

Visit [go.vitals.com/vitals/doctor/claim/](https://go.vitals.com/vitals/doctor/claim/) and complete the fields truthfully. Use the comments area to identify the physician profile you intend to claim and, when possible, include the exact Vitals profile URL.

A useful comment might read: "I am an authorized practice manager for Dr. Example. We would like to claim the existing physician profile at [profile URL] and confirm the current practice location and contact details." Do not include protected patient information.

### Step 3: Follow the Verification Instructions

Treat submission as a request, not as confirmation that the listing is claimed. Watch the official practice email for next steps. If you are asked to establish that you represent the physician, supply the documentation the platform requests through its official process.

The exact dashboard fields or verification methods may change. Rather than following an outdated third-party screenshot, follow the current instructions from Vitals.

### Step 4: Review the Profile After Access Is Granted

Check the visible profile as a patient would. Confirm the name, specialty, address, phone number, location affiliations, and any photograph or description that can be edited. Vitals says claimed providers can update photographs and other data elements through its workflow.

Document what you submitted, what was approved, and the date of your last visual check. Changes do not help patients if they never appear on the public page.

## How Do You Correct a Wrong Practice Address or Phone Number?

Use a **record-specific correction request**. Include the doctor profile URL, the field that is wrong, the accurate replacement, and a verifiable practice source such as the relevant office contact page. For physicians who work at more than one location, state clearly which locations are current and which should no longer be associated with the clinician.

Vitals explains that it compiles data from sources outside the doctor's direct control. Therefore, fix the information at the source when appropriate as well. If the doctor's official practice website, hospital bio, or relevant professional registry is outdated, stale details may keep resurfacing elsewhere.

### A Practical Correction Log

| Field | What patients currently see | Correct value | Evidence | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Practice address | Former office | Current patient-facing office | Official practice location page | Submitted |
| Appointment phone | Retired phone line | Current scheduling number | Current contact page | Pending review |
| Specialty | Historical specialty label | Current accurate specialty | Physician biography | Needs confirmation |
| Provider status | Inactive | Actively practicing, if supported by facts | Licensing and practice evidence | Escalated |

This is an illustrative workflow, not a claim that Vitals has these exact status fields.

## Why Might Vitals Mark an Active Doctor as Inactive?

Vitals identifies two broad reasons: problems with the physician's active licensure or an inability to confirm an appropriate current practice address. A doctor can therefore have a valid license but still be affected by outdated or unverifiable location data. The platform's own FAQ directs an active physician with this problem to claim the profile so the listing can be updated.

Do not simply request the word "inactive" be removed without investigating the cause. Check licensure, current practicing status, and whether the associated address is genuinely accessible to patients. A private residential or former office address does not become a valid practice location just because it appears in an old database.

## Can a Doctor Remove Their Vitals Listing Completely?

**Not simply by opting out.** Vitals states that it generally lists actively licensed U.S. physicians using public-record information and does not remove or suppress entries merely because a physician requests it. Incorrect or outdated information, however, can be reported for correction through [Vitals support](https://www.vitals.com/feedback).

If a physician has retired, changed licensure status, or permanently stopped seeing patients at a location, report those facts with evidence. The correct goal is an accurate public record, not an unsupported deletion request.

## Does Being on Vitals Improve Local SEO?

An accurate Vitals record can give prospective patients another place to verify a doctor's identity, office details, and public-facing information. It is one component of a broader local presence, not a substitute for a clinic website or a Google Business Profile.

Avoid claiming that a Vitals citation by itself guarantees better Google rankings. Its practical value is clearer: fewer conflicting details, a more useful physician profile, and a public destination that people may find while evaluating care.

### How Vitals Fits With Other Medical Listing Sources

| Surface | Primary identity | Main job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vitals | Physician and associated practice information | Doctor discovery and accurate profile information |
| Google Business Profile | Eligible clinic location or individual practitioner | Search and Maps visibility, calls, directions, reviews |
| Official practice website | Clinic and doctor pages under the practice's control | Authoritative service, team, location, and appointment information |
| Relevant professional registry | Licensed or registered provider facts | Professional identification and credential reference |

Do not force all four surfaces into identical wording. A doctor's name on Vitals and a clinic's trading name on Google can both be correct when they describe different real-world entities.

## A Repeatable Workflow for Multi-Doctor Practices

A group with ten clinicians needs more than ten one-time claims. Assign a responsible owner for each record and maintain one master spreadsheet with the physician name, primary location, secondary locations, profile link, current contact details, claim status, correction requests, and last checked date.

Review the list whenever a physician joins, moves, changes schedule, or leaves the practice. Perform a routine periodic check of the most important public profile fields. Prioritize live patient-impacting errors—wrong address, disconnected phone, incorrect active status—ahead of cosmetic fields.

### Example: A Dermatology Group Moves Offices

Suppose a dermatologist joins a two-location group and stops treating patients at a previous clinic. The group should update the physician's own bio page, confirm the new appointment number, review the Vitals physician record, and request removal of the old practice association if it is inaccurate. The group's Google profiles should be audited separately under Google's eligibility rules.

The lesson is to update the **relationship between doctor and location**, not simply to paste a new company name everywhere.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Claiming the wrong person.** Similar names require careful identity checks.
- **Treating a physician record as a business listing.** The doctor and the clinic are different entities.
- **Using an old office phone.** A live listing with an unusable phone is a patient-service problem.
- **Assuming edits synchronize across websites.** Each source may need its own correction.
- **Inventing specialties or credentials.** Only publish claims supported by the doctor's real qualifications.
- **Promising instant approval.** Vitals may request additional information before allowing updates.
- **Requesting blanket deletion.** A factual correction is different from a preference not to be listed.

## What Should You Do After Claiming the Listing?

Search for the doctor and clinic from a patient perspective, open the live Vitals profile, and compare each visible field with the practice's current location page. Keep a change log and a responsible owner. Repeat the review after every physician onboarding or departure.

For healthcare local SEO, **accuracy beats listing volume**. Five trustworthy records are more useful than fifty uncontrolled entries that send patients to the wrong office.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is it free to claim a physician profile on Vitals?

Vitals offers a public physician-claim form. That page does not list a claiming fee, but any optional services or commercial arrangements should be verified directly with Vitals rather than assumed. Start with the [official claim form](https://go.vitals.com/vitals/doctor/claim/).

### Can an office manager claim a Vitals profile for a doctor?

Yes, Vitals explicitly permits physicians **or their staff members** to claim profiles. The staff member should be genuinely authorized by the physician or practice and should use accurate, verifiable contact details.

### Can I create one Vitals listing for a medical group instead of each doctor?

Vitals includes group-practice discovery, but its public claim route is specifically framed around a physician profile. Check existing records and contact Vitals for group-practice questions rather than assuming the physician form creates a generic company listing.

### How long does a Vitals profile update take?

Vitals does not state one universal completion time on its public physician claim form. Submission, verification, and visible publication are separate events. Track each stage and follow up through official channels if the information remains wrong.
