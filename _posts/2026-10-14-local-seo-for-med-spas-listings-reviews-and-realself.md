**Local SEO for med spas starts with a verified, accurate Google Business Profile, a useful location-specific website, consistent business information, and a trustworthy review process.** RealSelf can play a separate role for aesthetic procedures and qualified providers, but it is not a replacement for Google Maps visibility. The strongest strategy connects three different questions: Can someone find the med spa nearby? Can they verify its services and credentials? Can they trust the experience without being misled about results?

A med spa has a tougher version of the usual local SEO problem. It is a local consumer business, but many services involve medical claims, licensed professionals, sensitive patient information, and treatment expectations. Optimizing a profile without taking these issues seriously can create bigger problems than a weak ranking.

This guide explains which listings to prioritize, how RealSelf fits, how to handle reviews safely, and what to measure without treating rankings as the only goal.

## What Makes Local SEO for Med Spas Different?

Med spas are discovered through both **location searches** (such as "med spa near me") and **procedure research** (such as a treatment name, suitability, recovery time, or provider credentials). These are not identical journeys.

A nearby customer may start with Google Maps, compare hours and reviews, and check appointment availability. A procedure-focused patient may spend more time researching qualified providers, before-and-after results, potential limitations, and what the consultation covers.

That distinction should shape your local search strategy. Do not write every page and listing as though the only desired action is a quick booking. For medical or invasive services, informed consultation and accurate expectations matter.

### The Three-Layer Visibility Model

| Layer | Patient question | Core assets | Main priority |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Location discovery | "Who offers this near me?" | Google Business Profile, Maps, location page | Accurate location and service data |
| Provider evaluation | "Who is qualified to perform it?" | Practitioner biographies, credentials, RealSelf where eligible | Clear provider identity and expertise |
| Trust and conversion | "What will this be like?" | Authentic reviews, FAQ content, transparent consultation details | Evidence, privacy, realistic expectations |

A campaign that only buys directory placements may improve the number of places a business appears without answering the medical questions that influence a patient’s decision.

## Which Listings Should a Med Spa Prioritize?

Start with the surfaces that serve real discovery or verification needs. More directories are not automatically better. A list of 100 inconsistent citations can create confusion about a clinic's location and booking number.

### 1. Google Business Profile

For an eligible patient-facing med spa location, Google Business Profile is the foundation of local Search and Maps management. The clinic should claim or add its accurate business profile, choose categories that describe what it actually does, and maintain correct contact and location information.

Use the business's real-world name—not a string of treatment keywords. Google's [business representation guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) also prohibit fake office locations, unnecessary duplicates, and misleading naming practices.

### 2. Your Own Website and Location Pages

Your site should remain the authoritative home for what the practice offers, who performs treatments, how consultations work, and where the clinic operates. A site that only contains hero images and an appointment button misses the chance to answer meaningful local questions.

Useful pages include a distinct location page for each clinic; a provider page for relevant licensed professionals; treatment pages covering candidacy, considerations, and consultation expectations; and a contact page with accurate address and opening hours. Put clinical claims through an appropriate review process.

### 3. RealSelf Where the Provider and Services Are a Fit

[RealSelf](https://www.realself.com/dr/claimprofile) is an aesthetics-focused discovery and education platform. It offers an official profile-claim workflow for providers and practices and says a free profile can support reviews, photos, videos, and participation in educational Q&A. It also offers optional paid upgrades.

RealSelf emphasizes physician credential checks and provider standards. It should therefore be considered a **provider-eligibility and treatment-fit decision**, not a place to submit every employee or every med-spa branch as though all profiles are interchangeable. Verify the available profile type and platform requirements before applying.

### 4. Relevant Local and Professional Sources

Keep authoritative business information accurate on applicable local business directories, professional directories, licensing sources, relevant chamber or community sites, and industry listings. The right mix depends on your services, licensed professionals, and location.

A clinic offering only nonmedical spa services may not fit the same specialist directories as an aesthetic practice led by credentialed medical providers. Do not create misleading medical listings to gain extra citations.

## How Should You Optimize a Med Spa Google Business Profile?

The goal is to make the real clinic easy to identify and contact. It is not to stuff every treatment name into every editable field.

1. **Confirm the location is eligible.** It should be a genuine patient-facing business that meets Google's in-person eligibility and location rules.
2. **Use the real business name.** Match how the clinic represents itself on signage and other real-world materials.
3. **Choose a truthful primary category.** Pick the most specific available category that actually reflects the core business, then use only relevant additional categories.
4. **Use the correct phone and website.** Route people to the clinic or location they intended to contact, ideally to a relevant local landing page.
5. **Maintain accurate hours.** Separate normal business hours from special hours, and do not advertise hours when no one can contact or visit the location if that conflicts with policy.
6. **Add real photos.** Exterior, entrance, reception, staff, and treatment-room images can help a visitor recognize the premises. Avoid deceptive or unauthorized patient imagery.
7. **Use actual services.** Describe genuine offerings plainly and respect Google content restrictions for regulated products and medical claims.
8. **Keep ownership in-house.** Give an agency manager access instead of transferring primary ownership as a default.

Google can change available category names and editing features. Select from the categories actually offered in your dashboard rather than relying on an old third-party list.

## What Should a Strong Med Spa Location Page Include?

Every location page should answer who, where, what, and how to book—without requiring someone to navigate five menus. A good structure is:

- A clear location name, address, phone number, and booking route.
- Practical access information: parking, building entrance, landmarks, or transit where useful.
- Services actually offered at that location, not a cloned list of everything the chain sells.
- Providers who genuinely practice there, with links to individual biographies.
- Photos that reflect the real premises.
- A short explanation of consultation, eligibility assessment, and appointment process.
- FAQs with patient-centered answers and appropriate medical caveats.
- Relevant internal links to service, safety, financing, or aftercare information where accurate.

### Example: Two Med Spas With Different Service Menus

Suppose a group runs one clinic focused on injectables and another with a broader laser-service menu. Copying the same treatment descriptions onto both local pages implies services are interchangeable even if qualified staff or equipment differ. The better approach is to keep the shared brand consistent while giving each location its true inventory and provider team.

A location page can still be attractive and concise. What matters is that someone deciding whether to travel to that branch can get a correct answer.

## Where Does RealSelf Fit in a Med Spa's SEO Plan?

RealSelf is most useful as a specialized destination for people comparing aesthetic treatment options and providers. Unlike a generic city directory, it can give space for patient experiences, before-and-after material, and educational provider content, subject to its rules and the provider's eligibility.

RealSelf's [claim page](https://www.realself.com/dr/claimprofile) currently states that claiming a profile is free and presents additional paid upgrade options. It also provides a [provider support contact](mailto:providersupport@realself.com). Its [provider standards](https://www.realself.com/company/standards) describe licensing checks, review moderation, and qualifications associated with the RealSelf Verified badge.

### How to Approach a RealSelf Profile

**First, identify the right entity.** Is the profile about the qualified physician or provider, the practice, or both? Do not assume a clinic marketing manager can create a separate medical profile for every brand keyword.

**Next, review eligibility and credentials.** Be accurate about licensing, board certification where applicable, qualifications, and the treatments each individual actually performs.

**Then build a useful profile.** Provide an authentic provider biography, current practice details, permitted imagery, and educational information that addresses realistic treatment questions. Do not suggest that an optional paid upgrade guarantees rankings, inquiries, or clinical outcomes.

**Finally, track a different kind of conversion.** A person who reads procedure information and books a consultation may require several research visits. RealSelf activity should be evaluated with its actual reporting and lead quality, not merely by counting profile views.

### Google Business Profile vs RealSelf vs Website

| Question | Google Business Profile | RealSelf | Practice website |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Helps with location discovery | Central use case | Can support provider discovery | Supports search and direct navigation |
| Shows clinic identity and hours | Yes, for eligible profiles | Depends on available profile fields | Fully controlled by practice |
| Communicates practitioner credentials | Practitioner profiles may apply | Provider-focused standards | Provider bio pages under practice control |
| Hosts reviews | Google Maps reviews | Aesthetics-focused review features | Testimonials subject to relevant rules |
| Shows treatment education | Limited listing and linked content | Procedure content and Q&A | Best place for structured, comprehensive information |
| Direct ownership of content | Platform-governed | Platform-governed | Practice-controlled |

These channels work best when they agree on facts but offer different levels of detail.

## How Should Med Spas Request and Respond to Reviews?

A good review process is repeatable, honest, and private. Google permits businesses to ask for genuine feedback but prohibits incentives for reviews and selective solicitation designed to produce only positive sentiment. The [FTC Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers) also addresses fake, deceptive, and certain incentivized reviews.

A simple process is to send a neutral review request to appropriate customers through a properly authorized channel, without filtering recipients based on expected rating. Do not offer a discount, treatment credit, or product in exchange for a Google review. Never ask staff, friends, or contractors to impersonate patients.

### Reviews Are Not Permission to Disclose Patient Details

If a patient publicly describes a procedure, the clinic should not assume it can publicly confirm that visit. The [American Medical Association](https://www.ama-assn.org/health-care-advocacy/administrative-burdens/are-physicians-prohibited-responding-online-patient) explains that physicians may respond to reviews but must protect patient information. The U.S. Department of Health and Human Services has documented enforcement actions involving healthcare providers disclosing protected information in replies to online reviews.

**Safer public response example:**

> Thank you for sharing your feedback. We take concerns seriously and invite you to contact our office directly so our team can review them privately.

Avoid: "You came in for Botox on Tuesday, and our records show you approved the result." That confirms sensitive information and argues the patient's case in a public forum. Have your compliance lead approve response patterns appropriate to your clinic's HIPAA obligations and applicable state laws.

### What About a Suspicious or False Review?

Flag content that violates the platform's policies and retain internal evidence through appropriate confidential processes. Do not publish the patient's chart, appointment history, or identifiers to refute it. Keep a documented internal escalation route for treatment safety concerns and privacy complaints; a public review thread is not the right venue for clinical resolution.

## Are Before-and-After Photos Good for Local SEO?

They can help explain what a qualified provider offers, but only when used responsibly. Before-and-after photos are especially sensitive because they can identify individuals and communicate health information. **Obtain appropriate, documented authorization when required**, follow clinic privacy procedures, and comply with the relevant platform and medical-advertising rules. Do not assume a general intake consent automatically authorizes public marketing use.

Photos should not misrepresent results through inconsistent lighting, angles, filters, or retouching. Explain when outcomes vary and avoid implying that one image predicts what a different patient will experience. Follow [HHS guidance on PHI and marketing](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/faq/marketing/index.html) where HIPAA applies.

## Which Med Spa Content Topics Deserve Dedicated Pages?

Prioritize topics that support informed local decisions. Examples include the consultation process, provider qualifications, realistic downtime and follow-up considerations, differences between treatment options, and which services are available at which clinic.

Avoid publishing dozens of identical city pages that change only the place name. Likewise, resist unsupported declarations such as "risk-free," "guaranteed results," or "the best in town." Medical accuracy and transparency are more valuable than keyword repetition.

### A Useful Content Architecture

- **Location page:** where the service is available and how to visit.
- **Treatment page:** what the procedure is, who may be a candidate, and what to discuss with a clinician.
- **Provider page:** credentials, scope of practice, and actual locations.
- **FAQ page or section:** consultation, appointment logistics, pricing transparency when appropriate, and preparation questions.
- **Aftercare resource:** information reviewed by a qualified clinician and clearly distinguished from individualized medical advice.

Each page should have one primary purpose. Cross-link related pages naturally instead of repeating the same paragraph everywhere.

## How Do You Measure Whether Med Spa Local SEO Works?

Track outcomes across the path from discovery to consultation rather than treating map ranking as the only indicator. Google Business Profile performance, Search Console queries, location landing page sessions, booking-form completion, qualified calls, and show-up rates can each add a piece of context.

| Metric | What it tells you | What it does not prove |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Maps visibility and interactions | Whether people find and engage with a profile | That every interaction becomes a patient |
| Location page search traffic | Demand and landing-page reach | Clinical suitability or patient satisfaction |
| Qualified consultation requests | Business relevance of inbound interest | That search alone caused every booking |
| Review volume and themes | Feedback availability and recurring concerns | Guaranteed service quality |
| RealSelf leads or inquiries | Platform-specific contribution where tracked | Automatic incremental revenue |
| Incorrect-listing count | Operational data health | Direct ranking uplift |

Use privacy-conscious, legally compliant measurement. Be especially careful with trackers, forms, and health-related user information; the fact that a marketing tool is common does not make its data handling appropriate for healthcare.

## A 30-Day Implementation Plan

**Week 1: Identity and listings.** Audit clinic names, addresses, numbers, website URLs, and ownership across Google and relevant directories. Resolve the most harmful wrong details first.

**Week 2: Location and provider pages.** Improve the two pages most likely to help people reach the correct clinic. Confirm provider credentials and clinic-specific services.

**Week 3: Reviews and RealSelf.** Establish a non-incentivized feedback request workflow, approved public response guidance, and evaluate RealSelf eligibility for appropriate providers.

**Week 4: Quality control and measurement.** Check public listings after updates, test booking links and phone routing, and establish baseline Search Console, GBP, and inquiry metrics. Set owners for recurring review.

This sequence is a management framework, not a promise of first-page rankings in a month.

## Common Mistakes Med Spas Should Avoid

- Adding treatment keywords to the legal or displayed clinic name when they are not part of the real-world name.
- Creating location pages for offices that do not actually exist or serve patients.
- Publishing credential claims without checking the professional's scope of practice.
- Treating RealSelf as just another bulk directory citation.
- Offering rewards for Google reviews or asking only happy patients to review.
- Responding publicly with visit, procedure, or billing details.
- Using before-and-after images without appropriate permission or with misleading presentation.
- Measuring success only by keyword position rather than qualified appointments and data accuracy.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is RealSelf necessary for every med spa?

No. It is most relevant when the clinic's qualified providers and aesthetic services fit the platform's audience and eligibility requirements. An accurate website and Google Business Profile generally deserve attention before optional specialty-platform investments.

### Should every injector have a separate Google Business Profile?

Not automatically. Google has specific rules for individual practitioners who are public-facing and can be contacted directly at an eligible location. A business should assess each person's actual status rather than create profiles for every service or staff role. See [Google's practitioner guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177).

### Can a med spa offer discounts for Google reviews?

No. Google's policies prohibit payment, discounts, free goods, or services in exchange for a review, a revised review, or removal of a negative review. Ask for genuine, unbiased feedback without incentives.

### Do med spas have to answer every negative review?

There is no blanket requirement to respond to every review. A thoughtful response can be appropriate, but patient confidentiality comes first. Use an approved public response that avoids confirming the reviewer is a patient and directs case-specific discussion to a private channel.
